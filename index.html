KPI Dashboard Builder

MMM760 Small Business Management — Week 9 seminar preparation

Build a one-page KPI dashboard for your practice business. Choose your business type, then select three to five metrics that will tell you most clearly whether the business is on track. As you select each metric, you will get plain-English feedback to help you decide whether it earns its place on the dashboard.

Why three to five. A dashboard with twenty metrics will not get reviewed every week. A dashboard with three to five carefully chosen metrics will. The skill this activity builds is choosing well, not measuring more.

You must be able to defend every metric you choose. The metrics are grouped into three categories — leading indicators, contextual metrics, and metrics often considered vanity metrics. The categories are starting points, not rules. A metric that is a vanity metric for one business may be a real leading indicator for another. If you select a contextual or vanity metric, you are not penalised — but you must explain in plain language why it is a real revenue indicator for your specific practice business. Your peer reviewer will press on this. Generic rationales like "it builds brand awareness" will not survive the review.

What to do with it. Complete the dashboard and export it to Word. Bring the file to seminar — it is the artefact your partner will review in the peer preparation activity, and your reflection on that review goes into your Assessment 3 memo.

Click a metric to add it to your dashboard. Click again to remove. Aim for three to five metrics — you cannot select more than five. As you select each metric, you will see plain-English feedback. Every metric you select needs a defence. Leading indicators are the easiest to defend because they connect directly to revenue. Contextual and vanity metrics can still earn a place — but you will need to explain specifically why, for your business, the metric is a real revenue signal. 4. Action prompt

A measurement without a question or decision attached to it is just bookkeeping. Write one question or action prompt the dashboard would force you to ask each week. This is what makes the dashboard useful rather than ornamental.

e.g. "Conversion rate dropped from 22% to 15% — what changed in the booking process this week?" — or — "Repeat rate is climbing — what are we doing right that we should keep doing?"
